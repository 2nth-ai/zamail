What happened on 12 and 13 November

Microsoft 365 had a global outage that began on Thursday and ran into Friday. Reports peaked just after 20:00 South African time, and Microsoft was still restoring service the next morning.

Email was not the only thing that stopped. Teams and SharePoint were on Microsoft's list too, so the usual plan of "message people another way" failed at the same time.

  • 130,000Almost this many reports of Outlook problems on Downdetector in the United States alone, at the peak just after 20:00 South African time on Thursday.
  • Not only emailMicrosoft's list of affected services included Exchange Online, Teams and SharePoint Online.
  • EX1550543Microsoft's incident number. Microsoft said it had no evidence of malicious activity and would publish a report on the cause.
  • Courts, visasA United States federal appeals court was affected, and New Zealand's immigration agency paused visa processing.

Source: Newsweek, 13 November 2026, reporting Microsoft's statements and Downdetector figures.

Proven on a real Microsoft 365 mailbox

The day after the outage we connected ZA Mail to our own Microsoft 365 mailbox, the one 2nth runs its business on, and let it copy everything.

The mailbox now exists twice: once at Microsoft, and once on a server in Johannesburg that we control. We read it in a browser at an address of our own, with a password and second factor that Microsoft has no part in.

Nothing changed for the mailbox at Microsoft. No DNS record was touched and no mail was rerouted.

  • 47,633Messages copied from Microsoft 365, across Inbox, Sent Items and folders. None failed. We compared the count in every folder on both sides.
  • 10 minutesHow often new mail is copied across. The copy has run on this schedule since the first full copy finished.
  • 7,000 an hourRoughly the speed of the first full copy. A large mailbox takes an afternoon, not a migration project.
  • One small serverTwo processor cores and 4 GB of memory, in Google Cloud's Johannesburg region. Mailbox, webmail and scheduled jobs all run on it.

What this does not prove yet. This pilot copies mail from Microsoft and lets us read it. It does not yet receive new mail directly or send mail during an outage. Those parts pass our tests against a stand-in for Microsoft 365 and are the next stage of the pilot.

Pilot run on 14 and 15 November 2026. One mailbox, one organisation: ours.

How the full standby keeps mail running

ZA Mail does not replace Microsoft 365. Your staff keep using Outlook every day. In the full standby, ZA Mail sits in front, keeps a second copy, and is ready the moment it is needed.

  1. On a normal day

    Mail for your organisation arrives at ZA Mail first. It passes every message to Microsoft 365 unchanged and keeps a copy in its own mailbox for that person.

  2. When Microsoft is down

    ZA Mail keeps receiving. Staff open ZA Mail in a browser or phone mail app, find their recent mail already there, and read and send as usual. Nothing is switched over.

  3. When Microsoft is back

    Mail that was held is delivered to Microsoft 365. Mail your staff sent from ZA Mail is copied into their Outlook Sent Items. Everyone goes back to Outlook.

Sign-in that does not depend on Microsoft

ZA Mail has its own passwords and second factor. When Microsoft's sign-in service is the part that fails, your staff can still get in.

Your mail stays in South Africa

Run it in any local data centre or on your own hardware. The standby copy of your organisation's mail never has to leave the country.

Nothing to switch at 8 p.m.

Mail is already flowing to ZA Mail before the outage starts. There is no DNS change to make and nothing to wait for.

It only ever adds

ZA Mail never deletes mail and never alters a message on its way to Microsoft. A half-working Microsoft 365 cannot damage the standby copy.

What ZA Mail is built on, and why

The standby mailboxes run on Stalwart, an open-source mail server. We use its free Community edition, unmodified, from Stalwart's own published image.

What we use from StalwartWhat it does in ZA Mail
Mailbox storage with IMAP and JMAPHolds the second copy of every mailbox. Webmail and phone mail apps read from it.
Its own directory of accountsPasswords, a second sign-in factor and app passwords that live on your server. This is why sign-in keeps working when Microsoft's does not.
Sending, with DKIM signingLets staff send from ZA Mail during an outage, with mail signed for your domain.
Configuration from text filesEvery ZA Mail server is set up from the same plans in the repository, so a new one is built the same way each time.
Web administrationAccounts and settings are managed in a browser. We keep this off the public internet.

Why Stalwart

It is one program where older set-ups need four or five. A standby has to be simple enough to leave alone for months and still work on the night. It is written in Rust, which rules out a whole class of memory-safety faults.

Which licence

The Community edition is licensed under AGPL-3.0, an open-source licence. There is no licence key and no fee. We pin the version, currently 1.1.0, and move up only after our tests pass.

What we do not use

Stalwart also sells an Enterprise edition. ZA Mail uses none of it. One of its features is hosting many organisations in one server; ZA Mail gives each organisation its own separate server instead.

Where we are careful

Stalwart's 1.x line is new. In the full standby, the part that handles your mail on a normal day is Postfix, which has run mail servers since 1998. Stalwart only holds the copy.

Two more open-source programs complete it: Postfix receives mail and passes it on in the full standby, and Roundcube is the webmail your staff sign in to. ZA Mail's own code, which copies the mail, detects an outage and sets everything up, is licensed under Apache-2.0.

What is ready, stated plainly

ZA Mail is an early release. Here is exactly how far it has been proven.

Working and tested

  • Copying a full, live Microsoft 365 mailbox: 47,633 messages, none failed
  • Reading that copy in webmail on a production server in Johannesburg
  • Receiving mail for both systems
  • Holding mail while the primary is down, then delivering it once
  • Reading and sending from the standby, with signed outgoing mail
  • Copying outage-time mail back, with no duplicates
  • Outage detection that pauses background sync

Written, not yet proven live

  • ZA Mail in front of a real Microsoft 365 tenant
  • Sending from the standby on the public internet
  • Reading your staff list from Microsoft's directory
  • Automated deployment across two sites

Not built yet

  • Calendar and contact sync
  • Warming up sending addresses before an outage
  • Support for Google Workspace as the primary system

The first two items are proven on a live Microsoft 365 mailbox. The others run end to end in a local test environment against a stand-in for Microsoft 365.
